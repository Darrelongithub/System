# v1.8.2 — gap-fill exit correction (fills, not rules)

Date: 2026-09-20
Scope: fix how a **trade that is already open** is closed when the market gaps through its
stop or target. No entry rule, no filter, no threshold, no strategy parameter and no signal
changed; the trade set is byte-identical (same 2,286 triggers, same entries, stops, targets
and order types). The golden lock was re-baselined because the fix moves 52 exit prices and
one outcome — see §4 for why this is a correction, not a rule change.

Base: `main` @ `4cba607`
Branch: `arena/01a0aa9b-system`
Prior: `logs/v1.8-changes.md` (Filter F), `logs/v1.8.1-hardening.md` (safeguards + freeze)

---

## 1. The bug

`src/lib/analyzer/status.ts` resolved a take-profit or stop **only on an in-bar touch**:

```ts
const touched = (candle, level) => candle.low <= level && candle.high >= level;
```

If a bar **opens beyond** the level and never trades back to it, `touched` is false. That is
exactly what a weekend/holiday reopen gap looks like: price jumps from Friday's close to
Monday's open, and the level in between is never printed. The engine therefore:

1. left a position that the market had already taken out **alive**, and
2. when price came back weeks later, resolved it **at the declared level** — booking the full
   planned R instead of the price that was actually available.

The entry side of the same engine was already gap-aware (`gapFill` in
`strategies/spec-strategies.ts`, `fillPrice` in `strategies/turtle.ts`: "if the candle opened
beyond the trigger the real fill is the open"). Only the exit side was gap-blind, so the same
bar could fill an entry at its open and refuse to close a position at it.

### Evidence from the locked baseline

|                            |                                                                                                                         |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Row                        | `three-soldiers` short, signal 2025-12-20 00:00, entry 4337.89, stop 4346.02, target 4318.265                           |
| What the market did        | 2025-12-22 03:00 EAT bar opens **4354.28**, high 4365.61, low 4354.28 — the stop was gapped clean over and never traded |
| What the old engine booked | ignored that bar, "hit" the target on 2025-12-29 and recorded **+2.4139R (TP)**                                         |
| What actually happened     | the short was stopped out at the reopen: **4354.28 → −2.016R (SL)**                                                     |

One trade in the certified book was recorded as a winner when it was a loser, and 51 more were
priced at a level the market never offered.

## 2. The fix

`status.ts` gained `gapFillPrice(candle, level, side, kind)`: when a bar **opens beyond** a
tracked level, the fill is that bar's **open** — an open is the first tick of the bar, so it
decides even if the bar later trades back through the level (a triggered stop does not wait to
be filled at the level it gapped past). It is applied to:

- the generic post-fill path (`evaluateSetupStatus`), before any in-bar touch: a gap over the
  stop is decided first, then a gap past the target, then the existing TP-then-SL touch policy;
- the pre-fill path: a stop **gapped over before the entry ever filled** now expires the setup
  (it was already expired when the stop was merely touched);
- the Crabel ORB path: a post-fill bar opening beyond the protective/breakeven stop;
- the Donchian path: a reopen gap through the 5-day trailing channel;
- Turtle positions and hypothetical (skipped) S1 signals: the 2N stop and the 10/20-day
  trailing exit, mirroring the file's own entry-side `fillPrice` rule.

In every path the resolution bar, price and the reason are reported explicitly, e.g.
`SL hit at 2026-04-20 03:00:00 at 4753.69 (gap through 4822.67: the bar opened beyond the stop
and never traded it, so the fill is the bar's open)`.

## 3. Blast radius (measured, not estimated)

|                               | before              | after                                                 |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Trades                        | 2,286               | 2,286 (identical keys, entries, stops, targets)       |
| TP / SL / OPEN / NO_FILL      | 751 / 1,531 / 4 / 0 | **750 / 1,532** / 4 / 0                               |
| Total R                       | 541.3570458970024   | **527.6272857378555** (−13.73R)                       |
| Win rate                      | 32.91%              | 32.87%                                                |
| Rows repriced                 | —                   | 52 (17 targets gapped past, 35 stops gapped over)     |
| Outcome flips                 | —                   | 1 (the `three-soldiers` row above)                    |
| Live/actionable rows affected | —                   | none (all 4 PENDING/FILLED rows have untouched stops) |

Every moved row was verified independently: the new exit price equals the resolving bar's
open, the open lies beyond the barrier in the direction that triggers it, and the resolution
bar is not earlier than before. A separate re-implementation of the exit walk agrees with the
engine on **2,286/2,286** rows.

The direction of travel is honest, not flattering: targets gapped past now book _better_ than
planned R, stops gapped over book _worse_ than −1R, and the aggregate falls. R is the realised
multiple (actual exit price against initial risk), exactly as `attachOutcome` documents.

## 4. Why the golden lock was re-baselined

The lock is a fixture, and a fixture that replays a known false result is worse than no
fixture. The `three-soldiers` row was a certified "win" that lost about 2R; keeping it would
have propagated into forward validation, per-strategy statistics and any filter comparison
made against this book.

What did **not** change: the trigger set, Filter C, Filter F, RR rules, spread handling,
consume-after-RR, the strategy parameters and every entry/stop/target level. `status.ts` is
re-frozen in `tests/ruleset-freeze.test.mjs` (the test's documented path for a deliberate rule
change: run the suite, record it in `logs/`, re-freeze in the same commit), and it is now also
part of the golden generator's rule-hash provenance.

Historical records are **not** rewritten: `logs/v1.8-changes.md`, `logs/v1.8.1-hardening.md`
and the audit files keep the numbers they were written with, and
`tests/filter-optout-legacy.test.mjs` records both the old and the new hash of the pre-Filter-F
book so the correction is traceable.
