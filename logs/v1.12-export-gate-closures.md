# v1.12 — Export gate closures

The generator refused to export a 66-hour series because of a 131-bar hole:

```
📋 Weekend gaps: FAIL (267 weekend closures + 5 holiday closures accepted,
   1 unexpected long gaps; 270 total; unexpected:
   2025-04-14 13:00:00 -> 2025-04-17 07:00:00)
❌ Validation FAILED: Weekend gaps
❌ OHLC fetch error: Error: CSV export blocked — validation failed: Weekend gaps.
```

131 bars out of ~50,000 — 0.26% — killed the run. All of it lived in
`validateOhlcExport` (`src/lib/ohlc-generator.ts`). Three separate defects, none
of which was the obvious one.

## 1. The holiday allow-list was three dates

The closure classifier was:

```ts
if (dow === 6 || dow === 0) closureDates.push(`weekend:${key}`);
else if (mm === "01" && dd === "01") closureDates.push(`holiday:${key}`);
else if (mm === "12" && (dd === "25" || dd === "26")) closureDates.push(`holiday:${key}`);
```

New Year, Christmas and Boxing Day, plus weekends. Good Friday, Easter Monday,
Memorial Day, 4 July, Labor Day and Thanksgiving were all unknown.

The failure mode is specific: a closure that is a **single weekday** creates a
~48h gap containing no weekend at all, so nothing excused it and the export
failed. Good Friday 2025 (18 Apr) produced a gap with an empty `closureDates`.
Memorial Day 2025 failed identically. This is the opposite of the intended
"deliberately narrow" allow-list — the narrowness did not make the gate strict,
it made it wrong.

Replaced with a computed calendar in `marketClosureDays(year)`, cached per year:

- **Easter** — anonymous Gregorian computus (`easterSundayUtcDate`), verified
  against published dates 2023–2028. Good Friday and Easter Monday derive from
  it, so they cannot drift out of the list.
- **Rule-based US closures** — Memorial Day (last Mon May), Labor Day (1st Mon
  Sep), Thanksgiving (4th Thu Nov).
- **Fixed dates with weekend observance** — New Year, 4 July, Christmas, Boxing
  Day. A Saturday holiday observes back to Friday, a Sunday holiday forward to
  Monday. Without this, 4 July 2026 (a Saturday) was booked as a plain
  `weekend:` and the observed Friday 3 July was still billed as missing data.

Scope is deliberately the closures a 24/5 FX/metals venue actually takes, not
every US federal holiday. The gate's job is to recognise a *known* closure, not
to excuse an arbitrary weekday hole — "an ordinary weekday is never a closure"
is pinned as a test.

## 2. One unexpected gap was fatal regardless of scale

```ts
const weekendPassed = unexpectedLargeGaps === 0;
```

Binary. 131 bars failed it; so would one bar. Replaced with a missing-data
budget in `assessMissingDataBudget`:

```
budget = max(12 bars, 5% of exported rows)
```

The 12-bar floor is 6 hours — small enough that a short file still has to be
essentially complete, large enough not to trip on bar alignment. Tolerated holes
are still listed by exact timestamp in the report; the gate names what it lets
through.

## 3. A gap was "expected" if it merely *contained* a closure day

```ts
expectedClosure: closureDates.length > 0
```

This is the worst of the three, and it failed **open**. A missing *year* passed
validation, because it contained 104 Saturdays. The classifier never asked how
much data was actually absent.

Now each closure day is credited a whole session (`BARS_PER_SESSION_DAY` = 48
bars) and only the residue is billed:

```ts
residualMissingBars = max(0, missingBars - closureDayCount * 48)
```

A clean weekend bills 0. The baseline's ten-day hole bills 294. A year-long
hole still fails — now because of its size, not by accident.

## Why the budget is 5%

Calibrated on the locked baseline, which ships a real ten-day hole at
`2026-04-30 23:30 -> 2026-05-11 03:00` (`artifacts/baseline-xauusd-ohlc.csv`
lines 6194–6195): 486 missing bars, 4 closure days credited (192), **294
billed**.

**The earlier estimate of 3.02% for this hole was wrong, and under-counted the
constraint.** The baseline also bills **4 bars at every one of its 38
weekends**. The weekly closure is `[Fri 22:00, Sun 21:00)` UTC, but the
artifact's Mondays begin at EAT 03:00 (= Mon 00:00 UTC) rather than at the
00:00 EAT weekly open, so each weekend gap spans 50.5h against a 48h two-day
credit: 100 missing − 96 credited = 4 billed.

The true figure is therefore **446 bars of 9,578 exported rows = 4.66%**, not
3.02%. 5% is kept — it is the round number that clears it — but the baseline
now consumes **93% of its budget**, and that is much tighter than it looks.
Both numbers are pinned by tests so a future re-baseline cannot drift past the
limit unnoticed.

## Also fixed

`analyzeTimelineGaps` computed `deltaMinutes` from parsed timestamps and guarded
with `deltaMinutes < 24 * 60`. An unparseable timestamp yields `NaN`, and
`NaN < 1440` is **false** — so the guard did not fire and the scan fabricated a
phantom gap, credited it closure days, and pushed it into the bill. Now skipped
when the delta is not finite, matching the module's existing stance that an
unreadable timestamp must never be silently interpreted
(`isInsideWeekendClosure`).

## Report line

The user's 131-bar hole on a 37,760-row file now reports (closure counts are
that run's own, and are shown here in shape, not re-measured):

```
📋 Missing data: PASS (tolerated) (… weekend closures + … holiday closures
   accepted, … total long gaps; 131 billed bar(s), 0.347% of the file,
   budget 1888; holes: 2025-04-14 13:00:00 -> 2025-04-17 07:00:00)
```

The billed count, share and budget for that case *are* pinned by
`tests/ohlc-export-gate.test.mjs` (`131`, `budgetBars === 1888`), as is the
requirement that the hole is still named.

The failure key changed from `Weekend gaps` to `Missing data budget`, which
describes what is now being checked.

### Note on the original process

The gap analysis was extracted to an exported `analyzeTimelineGaps` as a pure
refactor first, and the full suite re-run (207/207 unchanged) to prove the
extraction was behaviour-neutral. Only then were the defect tests written. This
is why the first run showed real behavioural failures
(`Memorial Day 2025: must be credited, got []`) rather than merely reporting
that new functions did not exist.
